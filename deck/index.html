01
The problem
The CFO can't answer one question
“How much cash will be in the account in eight weeks?”

4 opcos · 4 systems

Snelstart, Exact, Gilde & an invoice register — none reconcile to one cash view.

Rain stops the roof

Wet, windy or freezing workdays halt roofing — work, milestones & invoices slip.

The dip lands later

Billing falls ~3–5 weeks after the bad weather, then catches up — hard to see coming.

…then debtors pay on terms

A further ~4-week lag — against fixed covenant floors, exactly in the wet season.

04
Forecast engine
How the forecast
engine works
1
Start from each opco's billing pace
Recent run-rate (8-wk median) + its seasonal pattern — busier summers, quieter winters.
2
Shift the timing for weather
A pile-up of bad-weather workdays pushes a share of billing later (high 25% · med 12%); it returns as catch-up.
3
Turn billing into cash on terms
Spread each invoice across debtor weeks — cash peaks ~4 weeks after billing.
4
Subtract the cost-out drivers
Materials 32 · subcontractor 18 · labour 22 · overhead 10 % of revenue (assumptions).
5
Compare closing cash to the floor
Flag the weeks where headroom gets tight vs the covenant / liquidity floor.
6
Keep a trace behind every euro
Each number links back to its driver, assumption and source transaction.
Pure TypeScript · live weather (~2 wks) then seasonal as the basis · 15 tests green.
